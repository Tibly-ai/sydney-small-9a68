From the top

A voice built in church pews, kitchens, and back rooms

I grew up in a walk-up in Jackson Heights, three flights up from my grandmother's kitchen, where the radio never turned off. She taught me hymns on an upright piano that was always a little out of tune, and I learned to sing loud enough to cover for it.

By nineteen I was hosting open mics in Harlem on nights I couldn't afford the subway home. I studied every phrase Nina Simone ever bent and every silence Sarah Vaughan ever left hanging. I wasn't trying to sound like anyone. I was trying to figure out what I actually had to say.

The lounge circuit taught me the rest. Six years of Tuesday and Wednesday sets in rooms with low ceilings and lower lighting, where you learn to read a crowd by the way they stop talking. My sound settled somewhere between old soul and torch song, brushed drums and a voice that likes to sit right on the edge of the beat.

These days I split my time between a residency downtown, private events across the five boroughs, and writing new material with a trio I trust completely. The rooms have gotten bigger. The songs still start the same way: quiet, honest, and a little slow to open up.
